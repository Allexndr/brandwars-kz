## Verbatim quotes

> "Hundreds of copycat sites tried to replicate The Million Dollar Homepage's success, but none of them did."

> "Pixels4all.com was one of the many MillionDollarHomepage copycats. The site's financial performance was dismal: 4,800 pixels have been sold, which gives an estimate of the total revenue of $9.60."

> "Tew's Pixelotto (created by Alex Tew himself) was similar to The Million Dollar Homepage, except that every pixel cost $2. However, the pixels on the new site didn't sell as well as on the original, and the winner only got $153,000 rather than the expected $1 million."

> "The idea of selling pixels died around 2006, meaning the vast majority of these clones had essentially failed within the first year after the original's launch."

## Relevance

Directly answers Research Question 1 on clone survivorship. Provides concrete data: Pixels4all earned $9.60 on 4,800 pixels vs. MDH's $1.04M; even Tew's own Pixelotto clone yielded only $153k. Demonstrates the "novelty dies fast" principle—clones collapsed within 12 months. Answers the critical question: did clones sustain revenue? No—they failed almost immediately.
