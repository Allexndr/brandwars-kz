## Verbatim quotes
> "распространение в любой форме заведомо ложных, недостоверных сведений, связанных с деятельностью субъекта рынка"

(= "the dissemination, in any form, of knowingly false or unreliable information related to a market subject's activities")

## Relevance
This is the single most load-bearing legal text for research question 1. The statutory definition of discreditation has **two conjunctive elements**: (1) dissemination of "сведения" — **information/factual assertions** — about a rival, and (2) that information must be "заведомо ложных, недостоверных" — **knowingly false or unreliable**. A pure numeric-outbid ranking display shows only who paid more; it asserts no fact about either business's products, conduct, or quality at all, true or false — there is no "сведения" about the rival's activities being disseminated, only a record of the platform's own transaction (who paid the platform how much). Because the element of false/unreliable factual assertion is structurally absent, a neutral pay-to-rank display appears to fall outside Art. 181 on its face. This reasoning would NOT extend to a design where the losing party is described with claims about their business ("worse," "lower quality," etc.) — that would supply the missing "сведения" element instantly. It also does not, by itself, resolve the separate "visual battle framing" question (an image/label implying defeat rather than a factual claim) — see source 15 for that analysis via the honor/dignity/business-reputation standard, which requires the impugning content to be both about the party and **untrue** to be actionable.
