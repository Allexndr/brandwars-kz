# 09 — Fantasy Sports: Pay-to-Boost & Leaderboard Entry Fees

- URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/16184742.2024.2301970 & https://www.a3logics.com/blog/fantasy-sports-app-revenue-strategies/
- Accessed: 2026-08-27
- Source type: academic + industry guide
- Credibility (1-5): 4 (peer-reviewed study + DraftKings case documented)
- Recency: 2024-2026
- Bias notes: monetization-focused lens; successful models highlighted

## Verbatim quotes

> "Fantasy sports platforms generate revenue directly from users through entry fees or subscriptions, with portions going toward the prize fund, which can range from a few cents to several hundred dollars."

> "Gamification aspects such as leaderboards, prediction contests, and fan challenges increase user engagement and session durations."

> "DraftKings offers real-time stats, dynamic leaderboards, and high-stakes contests, and monetizes through paid contest entries, brand partnerships, and ads."

> "Consumers spent more in fantasy sports applications if their rate of gaining points was low and their rate of interactivity with the platform was high. Leaderboard progress had no significant relationship with in-app spending."

> "A user who checks scores, updates lineups, follows live games, and tracks leaderboard movement throughout the week is easier to monetize than a user who opens the app once a week."

## Relevance

Answers Research Question 3 on competitive-spend mechanics. Fantasy sports use dynamic leaderboards + paid entry tiers + real-time rank updates to drive spending. Counterintuitive finding: merely seeing leaderboard progress does NOT increase spend; high interaction + slow point gain + frequent checking DOES. Application: topbrands.kz success depends not just on leaderboard visibility, but on keeping businesses engaged (frequent checking, tight margin between 1st and 2nd place to encourage re-bidding). Prize-pool structure (entry fee split) also proven model.
