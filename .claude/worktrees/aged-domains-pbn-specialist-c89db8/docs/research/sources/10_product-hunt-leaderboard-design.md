## Verbatim quotes

> "Product Hunt's daily leaderboard shows a product's position on launch day, serving as a core metric for measuring launch day success. The platform also features weekly and monthly leaderboards that indicate sustained performance and long-term impact."

> "Product Hunt tracks 'friend voters' from a user's network, which signals social network effects on product success. This demonstrates how the platform incorporates social competition into its voting system."

> "Leaderboards are ranked displays showing how users perform against each other on specific metrics, and can be global, segmented by groups, or filtered by time period. Leaderboards tap into our competitive nature and desire for status."

> "Global all-time leaderboards can have a failure mode where top positions get locked in by early committed users, and the leaderboard stops functioning as a motivational mechanic for others. This is an important consideration for how platforms structure their competitive features."

## Relevance

Answers Research Question 3 on competitive-spend UX patterns. Product Hunt's multi-tier leaderboards (daily/weekly/monthly) ensure that new entrants always have achievable goals (don't just see "locked" positions). Key design lesson: static all-time leaderboards kill motivation. Application to topbrands.kz: should consider time-based tiers (weekly champions, monthly leaders) to prevent rank-locking and maintain spending incentive for new businesses entering the platform.
