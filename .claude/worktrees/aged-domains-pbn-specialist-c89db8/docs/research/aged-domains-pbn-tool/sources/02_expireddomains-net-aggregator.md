# 02 — ExpiredDomains.net: free listing aggregator, no built-in quality/spam vetting

- URL: https://www.expireddomains.net/
- Accessed: 2026-08-27
- Source type: primary
- Credibility (1-5): 5 (official product homepage, directly fetched)
- Recency: undated, evergreen product page (live/current as of access date)
- Bias notes: Vendor's own homepage — will describe itself favorably, but its claims are about data coverage/filters (verifiable, unglamorous) rather than quality claims, so overstatement risk is low. The absence-of-feature finding below is my own observation of what is NOT present, not a vendor quote.

## Verbatim quotes

> "Thousands of domain names expire every day. The reasons are different. Some owners forgot to renew the domains, some just don't want them anymore or they moved on to other projects."

> "Number of Backlinks, Archive.org Birth Date and a lot more"

Recent filter additions cited on the page: filters for "only with WBY," "only without WBY," nameserver field status, and OR-combination logic for dictionary matches. The site tracks "700+ different Top-Level Domains" (per search-index description, corroborated by the fetched page's TLD-filter breadth) and is described on-page as helping users "separate the good ones from the bad ones" through technical/metric filtering.

## Relevance

ExpiredDomains.net is the largest free aggregator of expiring/dropped-domain listings and already surfaces Majestic CitationFlow/TrustFlow, Wayback ("WBY") presence, and backlink counts as filterable columns — so any new product competing purely on "list of aged domains with metrics" is competing against a free incumbent with the broadest TLD coverage. Directly bearing on **H1**: the fetched homepage content contains **no mention of spam, adult-content, casino, or pharma domain filtering, and no red-flag/quality scoring** — it is a pure technical-metrics aggregator, not a vetting tool. This is the clearest evidence in this workstream that the specific job (screening for spam/casino/adult/pharma/hacked history) is a distinct, unautomated layer on top of what even the biggest free tool provides — supporting the idea that a real gap could exist, though SpamZilla (source 01) already partially fills it for paying users.
