## Verbatim quotes

**Instant Domain Search:**
> "As you type, the tool checks millions of records and displays availability in less than 100 milliseconds."
Pricing: "Completely free." Positioned for "speed-focused brainstorming with multi-TLD comparison views" — a domain-availability checker, not an aged-domain quality/vetting tool.

**GoDaddy Auctions:**
> "If a domain was originally registered through GoDaddy and the owner forgets to pay the renewal fee, it ends up here."
Pricing: "Membership fee is $4.99/year." Feature: "Exclusive Inventory" of high-authority domains before public release.

**Register Compass:**
> "It aggregates listings from GoDaddy, NameJet, SnapNames, and more."
Pricing: "$37/month | Quarterly: $97/quarter | Yearly: $347/year | Lifetime: $999." Feature: "Power Search feature that lets you combine dozens of filters."

## Relevance

This rounds out the competitive landscape beyond the four named tools in the brief: Instant Domain Search is a pure availability-checker (not a competitor for vetting), GoDaddy Auctions is a marketplace/source (not a vetting layer), and Register Compass is another metrics-aggregation search tool in the same mold as DomCop/ExpiredDomains.net (filter-and-search across auction sources, no stated spam/casino/adult/pharma content classification). None of the additional tools found in this broader sweep claim to do content-category red-flag detection beyond what SpamZilla already does — reinforcing that SpamZilla's SZ Score is the closest thing the self-serve market has to automated "spam/casino/adult/pharma" screening, and that the rest of the category is metrics search/filtering plus marketplace access, not judgment automation. This bounds the competitive-landscape picture for **H1**: a new entrant's differentiation opportunity, if one exists, is narrower than "find aged domains" (well-served, several tools, one free) and closer to "trustworthy automated judgment on red flags," which is the one claim no tool in this sweep makes with confidence.
