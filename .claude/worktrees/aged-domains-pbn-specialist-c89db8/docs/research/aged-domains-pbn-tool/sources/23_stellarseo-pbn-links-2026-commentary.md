> "In the past, PBN links could move rankings quickly. Today, most of them do not."

> "Modern Google algorithms frequently ignore these links entirely."

> "Rankings stay flat, visibility stalls, and money is lost quietly. That outcome is far more common than penalties."

> "Carefully constructed private networks can still move rankings in the short term, particularly in low-competition environments."

> "The issue is not whether they can work. The issue is how long the benefit lasts, how much risk is attached, and whether the same resources could produce stronger, safer results elsewhere."

> "Editorial links earned through outreach and relationships align with search engine guidelines and support long-term SEO growth."

## Relevance

This is 2026 industry commentary that directly addresses the brief's "is PBN a declining tactic" question, and it reframes the risk in a way that matters for H3: the dominant failure mode described here isn't a dramatic deindexing event anymore, it's Google's algorithms silently *ignoring* the links so the buyer gets no ranking benefit at all while still paying for domains, hosting, and content. For a SaaS whose value proposition would be "we help you find aged domains that will move rankings," this is arguably a worse market signal than penalty risk alone — it suggests a shrinking fraction of purchases actually deliver the outcome buyers are paying for, independent of whether the buyer ever gets caught.
