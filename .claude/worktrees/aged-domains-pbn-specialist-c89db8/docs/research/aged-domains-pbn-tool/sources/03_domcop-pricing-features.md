- Accessed: 2026-08-27
- Source type: primary
- Credibility (1-5): 5 (official pricing page, directly fetched)
- Recency: undated, evergreen pricing page (live/current as of access date)
- Bias notes: Vendor's own pricing page — prices are factual/verifiable, but feature framing (e.g., "most popular" label) is marketing positioning, not independent verification of value.

## Verbatim quotes

> "Newbie Plan" - "$816 /yr paid annually"

> "Power Plan" - "$1,152 /yr paid annually" (marked as "most popular")

> "Guru Plan" - "$1,416 /yr paid annually"

All three tiers list identical core access: "Access to Expiring Section," "Access to Expired Section," "Access to Archived Section," "Export Records," and "Personal Expired Domain Crawler" — differentiation is by export/crawl volume limits rather than by feature set.

From a second DomCop page (their own blog, fetched as part of source 05): "90+ SEO Metrics" pulled from multiple providers including Majestic, Moz, and SEMrush; DomCop "doesn't sell the domains itself; instead, it acts as a massive search engine that aggregates data from auction houses like GoDaddy, NameJet, Dynadot, and many more."

## Relevance

DomCop is the highest-priced of the aggregator-style tools researched ($68–$118/month billed annually, no free tier or trial), positioned as the deepest-data option (90+ metrics blended from Majestic/Moz/SEMrush/Ahrefs/Estibot) rather than as a spam/red-flag classifier — its pricing page makes no mention of spam, blacklist, or content-category (casino/adult/pharma) detection at all. This is useful for **H2** (data/cost feasibility): DomCop's price effectively reveals the market rate for aggregating third-party metric APIs at scale, which is a cost floor a new entrant reselling or replicating similar data access would need to beat or justify exceeding. It also reinforces the **H1** pattern from sources 01–02: even the most expensive self-serve tool sells metrics and search/filter power, not a finished "safe to buy" verdict.
