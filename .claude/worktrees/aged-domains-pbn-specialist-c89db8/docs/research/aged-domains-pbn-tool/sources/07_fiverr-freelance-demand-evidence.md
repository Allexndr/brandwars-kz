## Relevance

The **"Domain_web_seo" gig is the single most direct piece of evidence for H1 found across this entire workstream**: a freelancer is selling, for $10/domain, a manual research service explicitly built around running SpamZilla ("by spamzilla" in the gig title) for the buyer. This is not ambiguous — it shows a real, paying market for a human to operate an existing automated spam-detection tool (source 01) and apply judgment on top of its output, rather than buyers running SpamZilla themselves and trusting the score directly. Combined with six other similarly-priced ($5–$120) active gigs for the same underlying task, this establishes that low-cost human-in-the-loop expired-domain vetting is a real, current, multi-seller micro-market — strong support for H1's claim that buyers still pay humans rather than relying on tool output alone, though the low price points ($5–$30 for most gigs) also suggest the *manual* layer buyers are currently paying for is itself fairly shallow/commoditized, not a premium service — worth weighing against the higher-value Upwork postings in source 06 when sizing any product's addressable willingness-to-pay.
