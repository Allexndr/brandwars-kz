## Verbatim quotes
> "игра не должна быть ни совершенно случайной, ни совершенно определённой"

(= "a game must be neither entirely random nor entirely determined" — framing the doctrinal boundary condition for what counts as a game of chance at all)

Additional point captured via fetch (paraphrase, not exact quote): outcomes must hinge on factors "независимо от воли участников" (independent of the participants' will) to constitute prohibited gambling; risk is the critical differentiator separating games of chance from skill-based or merit-based competitive mechanisms, which are instead governed by ordinary civil-law provisions on commercial transactions (the article cross-references Russian Civil Code Arts. 444-449 on public tenders/auctions and Arts. 1062-1063 on games and bets as sitting in different legal categories).

## Relevance
Provides the doctrinal "why" behind the statutory test in source 16: gambling regulation exists specifically to police outcomes that are "независимо от воли участников" (outside participants' control/will). The described pay-to-rank battle mechanic is the textbook opposite case: the outcome (who holds top rank) is **entirely and exclusively within the participants' own will** — it is the direct, deterministic, immediately-knowable consequence of their own and their rival's voluntary payment decisions, with zero random or externally-determined component. This doctrinal framing, read together with Kazakhstan's own "основанное на риске" (risk-based) statutory language in the Gambling Business Law (source 16), reinforces a consistent, low-risk conclusion on research question 2: a deterministic, transparent, highest-bidder-wins ranking mechanic sits doctrinally and statutorily outside the "game of chance" category in both the general post-Soviet legal-theory framing and Kazakhstan's own codified definition.
