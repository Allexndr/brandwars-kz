## Verbatim quotes

> "Alex Tew, a 21-year-old student from Wiltshire, England, created this website in August 2005 to fund his university education. The site sold one million pixels arranged in a 1000×1000 grid for $1 per pixel (sold in 10×10 blocks minimum)."

> "The venture grossed $1,037,100 in five months. After costs, taxes, and charitable donations, Tew expected net income of $650,000–$700,000. The final 1,000 pixels sold via eBay auction for $38,100."

> "They popped up almost immediately; now there are hundreds of Web sites selling pixels. The copycats are all competing with each other. The idea only works once and relies on novelty." (Tew's direct observation on clones)

> "By 2017, approximately 547 of the original 2,816 links were dead (342,000 pixels worth $342,000), and 489 redirected elsewhere (145,000 pixels worth $145,000). By April 2019, roughly 40% of links suffered from link rot."

## Relevance

Directly answers Research Question 1 on the Million Dollar Homepage lineage: establishes the original earned $1M+ in 5 months, documents Tew's own observation that clones failed ("idea only works once"), and shows site decay over 14 years (40% link rot by 2019). Demonstrates the core principle that novelty-driven revenue is unsustainable once the original drops out of attention.
