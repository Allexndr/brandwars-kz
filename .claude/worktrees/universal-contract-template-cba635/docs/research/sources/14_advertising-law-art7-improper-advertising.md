## Verbatim quotes
Article 7, Part 1, subparagraph 1) — comparative/disparaging advertising:
> "содержит сравнение рекламируемых товаров (работ, услуг) с товарами (работами, услугами) других физических или юридических лиц, а также высказывания, образы, порочащие их честь, достоинство и деловую репутацию"

Article 7, Part 1, subparagraph 4) — humiliation/ridicule clause:
> "дискредитирует, унижает или высмеивает физические или юридические лица, не пользующиеся рекламируемыми товарами (работами, услугами)"

## Relevance
Confirms the exact two clauses most relevant to research question 1. Subparagraph 1) bundles two prongs: (a) comparison of advertised goods/services with a third party's goods/services, and (b) statements **or images** ("образы") that harm honor/dignity/business reputation — the express inclusion of "images" is the textual hook for the "visual battle framing" sub-question, since a purely visual "defeated"/"under attack" treatment could arguably fall under this prong even without a verbal product claim. Subparagraph 4) — the "discredits, humiliates, or ridicules" clause — is narrower than it first appears: on its face it applies to "физические или юридические лица, **не пользующиеся** рекламируемыми товарами" — i.e., persons who **do not use** the advertiser's product (the classic "if you don't buy X, you're a loser" ad trope). A rival business that is outbid in the ranking is not a "non-user of the advertised product" in that sense — this specific subparagraph's plain scope does not appear to target inter-competitor ranking display at all. That leaves subparagraph 1)'s "images...harming business reputation" as the operative risk vector for battle-framing, not subparagraph 4). See source 15 (Supreme Court standard) for how "harm to business reputation" is actually adjudicated — critically, it requires the impugning content to be **untrue**, which cuts strongly in favor of neutral, factually-accurate battle framing (e.g., "outbid," "position taken") and against framing that implies something false about the loser (e.g., implying they are inferior/defeated as a business rather than merely out-spent in this round).
