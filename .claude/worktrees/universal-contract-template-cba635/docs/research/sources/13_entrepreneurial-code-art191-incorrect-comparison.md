## Relevance
Second load-bearing text for research question 1. Art. 191 requires (1) a **public statement comparing one's own goods to a competitor's goods**, where (2) superiority is claimed **without documentary proof**. A pure numeric-outbid leaderboard makes no claim whatsoever about "товары" (goods/services) — it displays only who paid the platform more for a ranked placement slot, which is a fact about the transaction, not a comparison of product attributes. This is a structurally different act from comparative advertising ("our service beats theirs"). Even under a maximally aggressive reading that treated the rank display itself as an implicit "comparison," the second element cuts decisively in the platform's favor: the "documentary confirmation" gap the statute targets does not exist here — the payment amounts are the platform's own verifiable transaction records, not an unsubstantiated superiority claim. This supports a "likely outside Art. 191" reading for the opt-in bidding mechanic as specified (zero product/quality claims), though it has not been tested by AZRK or a Kazakhstani court against a gamified ranking product specifically — no such enforcement precedent was found in this research (see summary for that gap, flagged for lawyer sign-off).
