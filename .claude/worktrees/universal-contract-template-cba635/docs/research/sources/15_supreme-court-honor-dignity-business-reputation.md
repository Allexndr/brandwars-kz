## Relevance
This is the single most important source for the "visual battle framing" sub-question. It establishes two decisive filters that any battle/attack visual treatment would have to pass through before it could be actionable:

1. **Truth requirement**: information is only "порочащие" (defamatory/discrediting) if it is "не соответствующие действительности" — **untrue**. A "battle" UI that shows Business A currently holds the #1 spot because it paid more than Business B, and labels B's prior position as "outbid" / "under attack" / "lost the spot," is describing something that **actually happened** (a real, verifiable paid transaction). Truthful statements about a real competitive/paid event fall outside this defamation-type standard entirely, regardless of how dramatically they're dressed up visually — provided the underlying claim ("you got outbid") stays true and doesn't imply something false about the competitor's products, competence, or legality of conduct.
2. **Fact vs. value-judgment carve-out**: game-flavored language ("under attack," "defeated," "claim your throne") reads naturally as stylistic/rhetorical framing — an "оценочное суждение"/subjective expressive device — rather than a verifiable factual assertion about the rival's business. Per para. 11, such expressive framing is categorically not subject to судебная защита (judicial protection/challenge) under Art. 143 of the Civil Code, precisely because it cannot be tested for truth or falsity.

Net effect: battle/attack visual framing is **legally distinguishable** from the discreditation/incorrect-comparison risk, provided (a) the underlying fact displayed (who paid more) is true, and (b) the game language stays in the register of competitive/sporting metaphor ("outbid," "defeated in this round," "reclaim the spot") rather than tipping into value-laden claims about the rival's real-world business quality, honesty, or legality (e.g., avoid copy like "loser," "scam," "inferior," "can't compete" — those would read as substantive claims, not game flavor, and would reintroduce risk under Art. 181/191 and Advertising Law Art. 7(1) alike). This is a judgment call about UI copy and iconography, not a bright-line rule — flagged in the summary as one of the two points needing real lawyer sign-off before ship.
